Req2 Final Report: Claim Processing Validation

Requirement
Validate claim creation, modification, approval, rejection, closure, status transitions, payouts/settlements, and correct application of policy coverage.

Rules identified
- BR-01: Claims must support create, modify, approve, reject, and close; detailed constraints are unspecified.
- BR-02: Status transitions must follow an approved transition matrix; the matrix is not supplied.
- BR-03: Payouts and settlements must follow approved calculations; formulas and limits are not supplied.
- BR-04: Policy coverage must be applied; coverage terms and eligibility conditions are not supplied.

Fraud-testing findings
- Expected flag patterns detected: over-limit amount (RULE-001) and expired policy (RULE-002), both with synthetic data.
- CLM-004 shows current precedence: RULE-002 overwrites RULE-001 when both checks match. Intended precedence is undefined.
- CLM-005 shows there is no required-field validation in the current mock. Required-field requirements are undefined.
- CLM-006 shows duplicate IDs are accepted and appended. Duplicate/idempotency behavior is undefined.
- These are software test patterns only; no real-world fraud finding is made.

Blocked and missing coverage
The mock only implements POST /claims and GET /claims/:claimId. It has no modification, approval, rejection, closure, status-transition, payout, or settlement routes. Policy coverage/date rules are not implemented. Req2 also omits required fields, status transition rules, payout formulas, coverage terms, and rejection criteria, preventing definitive expected results for those cases.

Recommendations
1. Define required claim fields, duplicate/idempotency behavior, and precedence or aggregation for simultaneous fraud rules.
2. Specify allowed status transitions, validation and authorization rules, and expected responses for each lifecycle action.
3. Define policy coverage terms and payout/settlement calculations, including limits, exclusions, and boundary cases.
4. Implement the missing API operations and add positive, negative, and boundary tests for the blocked areas.
